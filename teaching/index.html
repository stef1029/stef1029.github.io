Open Science in Practice

Cambridge Open Lab summer school — Homerton College, University of Cambridge, 3–7 August 2026.

I taught engineering techniques for neuroscience on this week-long school, running practical workshops for PhD students in electronics design with Arduino and in CAD with FreeCAD — both chosen because they are free, open, and still usable once the week is over and everyone has gone back to their own lab.

The premise is one my own work has made me believe in. A great deal of experimental neuroscience is bottlenecked not on ideas but on instrumentation, and a researcher who can design a circuit board or a printed part is far less constrained in the questions they can ask. Nearly everything on my portfolio page exists because building it was cheaper, faster, or simply more capable than anything available to buy.

None of this came from an engineering degree. I arrived at the PhD from a biology background without knowing how to run a Python script, and everything since — circuits, firmware, CAD, the analysis pipelines — was picked up as it became necessary, usually because an experiment depended on something that did not yet exist.

Which is why I wanted to teach on it. Most of the room was roughly where I had been a few years earlier: capable scientists with a clear sense of the tool they needed and no obvious way in. Being able to hand someone a shortcut through the part I found hardest is a good use of a week.
